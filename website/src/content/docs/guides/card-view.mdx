**Settings → Card view**, or `/p/{projectId}/settings/card`.

A card is small on purpose, and what belongs on it is not the same for two teams. One board needs a
due date on every card; another needs the label colours and nothing else. This page says which it is.

It belongs to the **project**, not to you and not to a view, so everybody looking at that board sees
the same card. It **saves as you click** — there is no Save button, and the card on the right redraws
as you go, because it is the board's own card drawing your project's own tasks.

## What a row is

Every row is one thing a card could carry. Most are your properties. Five are parts a task has of its
own, and they behave the same way:

| Row             | What it draws                                     |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Task ID**     | `USH-14`                                          |
| **Title**       | The task title. It cannot move and cannot come off |
| **Description** | One or two lines of it, in the body                |
| **Checklist**   | A bar and `2/5`, or just `2/5`                    |
| **Comments**    | A speech bubble and a count                       |

<Aside title="Nothing here is a field on a task">
The five are the task row the board already has. Everything else on a card is a property somebody
defined, which is why deleting a property takes it off every card and adding one puts it back.
</Aside>

## The six places

Open a row's **On card** cell and the small card in the panel is the map. Click a box.

```
┌─┬──────────────────┐
│ │  L          R    │   header: two ends of one row
│E│  ────────────    │   the title, which never moves
│ │  [   BODY    ]   │
│ │  L          R    │   footer: two ends of one row
└─┴──────────────────┘
 └ the edge stripe
```

**Left** and **right** are the two ends of the same row, so a header can carry the priority colour on
one side and a face on the other. **Rows sharing a place sit in the order of the list** — move a row
with the arrows to put it in front of its neighbours.

**Take off the card** puts a row in *Not shown*. It keeps its place in the list, and the foot of the
page counts what is left: *10 of 12 on the card*.

### The edge stripe

A stripe of colour down the left of every card. **One property at a time**, and only a property that
has colours of its own — a select, a multi-select or a person. Ask a date for the edge and the box is
closed, and the row says why. Giving the edge to a second property takes the first one off the card,
and the panel says so before you click.

The edge is also what the **task panel** takes its band colour from.

## How a row reads

| Kind                       | Modes                                   |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Select, multi-select       | **Both** (dot and name) · **Colour** (a square) · **Text** |
| Person                     | **Avatar** · **Name** · **Both**        |
| Task ID, date, text, number, checkbox | **Plain** · **Boxed**        |
| Description                | **2 lines** · **1 line**                |
| Checklist                  | **Bar** · **Count**                     |

A multi-select draws one part per value, so three labels are three squares.

Asking an edge row for words moves it into the header, because a stripe has nowhere to put them.

## What a card actually draws

Only what the task holds. A task with no due date has a shorter footer than the one beside it, and
neither leaves a gap. A header with nothing in it is not drawn at all.

## Reset to default

Back to the card Ushabti drew before this page existed: the first select colour and the task ID in
the header, the assignee and the labels closing it, everything else in the footer, and the property
the board is grouped by left off, because the column already says it.

<Aside type="tip" title="The short way">
The **On card** toggle on the [Properties](/ushabti/guides/properties/) page is the same setting,
asked briefly: it takes a property off the card, or puts it back where its kind belongs.
</Aside>
